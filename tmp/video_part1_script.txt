## Section 2 — Your AI journey (~30 s)

**[B-roll: a screen recording of you opening an AI chat to ask about an unfamiliar term in an AI paper, then cutting to a running prototype 30 seconds later.]**

> "AI has measurably changed both the speed and the depth of how I learn.
>
> In my research, it has become the fastest way for me to get from 'I don't recognise this term' to 'I can hold my own in a conversation about it.' AI papers in particular use a lot of moving vocabulary — model, agent, tool-use, RAG, fine-tuning — and AI helps me close those gaps in real time.
>
> Just as importantly, AI has collapsed the distance between an idea and a working prototype. The phrase I keep coming back to is: the distance between *'I wonder if…'* and *'here's a running demo'* has never been shorter. That's true for me this summer — the project I'm about to show you would have taken me a team and a semester a few years ago."

---

## Section 3 — Something unique / fun fact (~35 s)

**[B-roll: a science textbook → a code editor → a history or culture book or museum visit, in quick cuts.]**

> "I think the most distinctive thing about me is that I don't stay inside my own major.
>
> In high school I studied science, and at university I study computer science — so I'm comfortable with STEM. But beyond that, I'm genuinely curious about the humanities: history, how people in other eras lived, why certain objects survived and others didn't.
>
> I like taking knowledge from different fields and trying to combine them instead of keeping them in separate boxes. This project is exactly that instinct in action: I used an AI agent to build a digital heritage museum — a place where anyone can upload the cultural artifacts they've seen, especially ones that aren't well protected or have already been forgotten. Computer science gave me the tool. The humanities gave me the reason."

## Section 4 — Career aspiration (~25 s)

**[B-roll: a city skyline, a modern office, people collaborating — quick, optimistic shots.]**

> "Why am I here, and what do I want from this experience?
>
> Two things. First, I want a full, 360-degree taste of what real IT work actually looks like — and to pick up industrial-grade, hands-on engineering skills along the way. I think of that as enriching my life experience, not just my résumé.
>
> Second, I want to expand my circle — to meet people in the industry, including the most experienced engineers, and to practice the kind of workplace relationships and communication that you can't learn from a textbook.
>
> With that, let me show you what I built."
